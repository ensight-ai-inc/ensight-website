The problem

Structural heart disease is common, treatable, and diagnosed too late.

Amyloid cardiomyopathy, left ventricular systolic dysfunction, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and valvular disease are diagnosed with echocardiography, cardiac MRI, or nuclear imaging. Those tests are ordered only once symptoms raise suspicion, so most patients are identified late, when disease is advanced and harder to treat.

Imaging is the bottleneck

Definitive diagnosis depends on tests that are expensive, capacity-limited, and concentrated in specialized centers, out of reach for many patients.

Symptoms are nonspecific

Conditions like ATTR cardiomyopathy are frequently missed or attributed to other causes, and they disproportionately affect Black individuals.

Therapies need patients

Effective treatments now exist, and they work best when started early. Clinical trials and approved therapies both depend on finding the right patients sooner.

Our approach

The ECG is everywhere. We teach it to see what imaging sees.

The 12-lead ECG is the most widely performed cardiac test: inexpensive, available in nearly every clinic in the world, and usually stored as a PDF or printed image. ECGvision models learn from hundreds of thousands of ECGs paired with echocardiography, cardiac MRI, and nuclear imaging, and they detect signatures of structural disease that are not visible to the human eye.

Format-independent No new hardware Designed for local deployment Works on smartphone photos

  1. Start with any ECG image

    PDF exports, scanned printouts, and photographs of ECGs across standard layouts. No raw-signal access or ECG-vendor integration required.

  2. Detect the hidden signature

    Proprietary deep learning models analyze the image and estimate the probability of a specific structural heart disease.

  3. Prioritize confirmatory testing

    Flag the patients most likely to benefit from echocardiography or advanced imaging, so scarce diagnostic capacity goes where it matters most.

  4. Scale from one patient to a population

    Analyze a single ECG at the point of care, or screen a health system’s ECG archive to surface undiagnosed patients.

FDA Breakthrough Device Designation · February 2024

ECGvision-ATTR: earlier detection of ATTR cardiomyopathy

Transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is a progressive, life-threatening, and widely under-diagnosed cause of heart failure. ECGvision-ATTR analyzes an image of a 12-lead ECG to detect the signature of ATTR-CM, helping clinicians identify who should go on to confirmatory imaging.

0.84
AUROC in internal validation across 44,123 patients
0.78–0.89
AUROC across 5 external multinational cohorts
0.76–0.91

0.24 → 0.66
Positive predictive value when AI-ECG is followed by AI-enabled echocardiography
Specific to amyloid, not just “abnormal.” Discrimination held among patients with ATTR-CM mimics, such as left ventricular hypertrophy or severe aortic stenosis without amyloid (AUROC 0.81).
A signal years before diagnosis. In a separate study, AI-derived ATTR-CM probabilities from ECGs and echocardiograms diverged between future cases and controls as early as 3 years before diagnosis.

Sources: Croon PM, et al. JAMA. 2026 (doi:10.1001/jama.2026.16785); Oikonomou EK, et al. Eur Heart J. 2025 (doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf450). Results describe the AI-ECG research models in retrospective and screening cohorts.

ECGvision-ATTR is an investigational device. It has not been cleared or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and is not commercially available. Breakthrough Device Designation does not constitute FDA clearance or approval.
